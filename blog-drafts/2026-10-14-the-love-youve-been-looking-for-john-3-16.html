Imagine being stranded in the wilderness for two days with no food. When you finally stumble back into civilization, someone hands you a single bite of bread. You will take it—of course you will—but even as you swallow it, you know it isn't enough. One bite isn't enough to fully satisfy that kind of hunger.

That picture captures something true about the human heart. Just as your stomach hungers for food, your soul hungers for love. And here's the honest truth: the love you receive from other people, even the very best of it, will fill you but never fully satisfy you. There is always something missing.

The Love We're All Searching For

Think about the relationships in your life. Some people love you until they really know you—until they find a failure they don't like, and someone who used to love you doesn't anymore. Others know your faults and failures and love you anyway. But even that love has limits. The people who love us best are still human. Sometimes they can't make the time to be with us. Sometimes they don't understand us. And there will be times they fail us, disappoint us, even sin against us.

What every soul is actually craving is to be both fully known and fully and eternally loved. No human relationship can carry that weight. Only one love can. This message centers on one short, familiar verse that points us straight to it:

"For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life." (John 3:16)

If you've heard that verse a hundred times, hear it like the first time. Two questions open it up for us: What is this love really like? And how should we respond to it?

What God's Love Is Like

There is a God-shaped space in every human heart that only His love can fill. And the way God chose to love us is staggering. He "gave his one and only Son"—He gave Jesus over to death on a cross in our place, for our sins.

Jesus wasn't put on the cross just by people. He wasn't forced onto it, and He didn't deserve it; He was no criminal. He lived the perfect life none of us has ever lived. He went there because the Father handed Him over to die the death we deserved. That is how much God loves you: He would rather die than live without you.

Here's a key distinction. Ask a search engine to define love and it will likely describe a feeling—a strong emotion toward another person. But by the Bible's measure, that's a superficial love. True love is measured by sacrifice: you know how much someone loves you by how much they're willing to sacrifice for you. Love that is only spoken and never shown isn't really love at all. God didn't just say He loves us; Jesus demonstrated it by sacrificing Himself in our place, for our sins.

God loves lost people

Notice who God loves in John 3:16—the world. That includes everyone. That includes you. And Scripture is clear about the condition of the world:

"All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God." (Romans 3:23)

"We all, like sheep, have gone astray, each of us has turned to our own way." (Isaiah 53:6)

Being spiritually lost isn't only about dramatic, obvious sins. At its root, it means we were made to love God with all our heart, mind, soul, and strength—and instead we've loved something or someone else first. A career, a relationship, an image, ourselves. We've all broken the first and greatest commandment. And yet you could never wander so far that God would stop loving you. God specializes in loving lost people.

His love is natural and eternal

Why can God love us like this? Because loving us isn't a chore He grinds through—it flows from who He is.

"God is love." (1 John 4:16)

Loving you is not a burden to Him; it's a joy. And His love doesn't expire: "His love endures forever" (Psalm 136:1). Before you were ever born, God saw your past, present, and future—every failure included—and still said, "I love that person. I'll die for them."

How to Respond to God's Love

God's love isn't meant to be merely heard about. It's meant to be received. And the verse tells us how: "whoever believes in him." The only right response to this love is belief in Jesus Christ as your Lord and Savior.

Faith isn't having everything figured out—if it were, no one would qualify. Faith simply means knowing, "I'm a sinner, and I need a Savior," and then trusting that Jesus truly lived, truly died, and truly rose so that you could be saved. It's falling into the Father's arms, trusting Him to catch you.

And here's the relief: you are not saved by the size of your faith but by the strength of the One you place it in. Pastor Josh remembers his very first flight, in eighth grade: a cross-country trip to the East Coast. He was sure the plane was going to crash, and every little bit of turbulence nearly gave him a panic attack, while people who fly every week sat with their earbuds in, sleeping right through it. Was the plane holding him any less than it held them? No. It held them all the same. The level of faith didn't matter; the object of faith is what mattered most. Even faith the size of a mustard seed, placed in the right Person, is enough.

And that offer is for anyone. Pastor Josh's favorite word in John 3:16 is whoever—any person, any time, any place, no matter what they've done. The apostle Paul once hunted and killed Christians, and God met him and saved him. Paul believed in Jesus, and God forgave him and made his life completely new. If grace reached him, it can reach you.

Our pastor didn't mince words about what's at stake: your response to God's love determines your destiny. Whoever believes will not perish, and the opposite is also true: whoever does not believe will perish. "The wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord" (Romans 6:23). Sin separates us from God, and to be separated from God is to be cut off from the source of life, like a phone unplugged from its charger and never plugged back in: eventually it dies. That separation is our choice, not His. God doesn't want anyone to perish; He wants all people to come to a knowledge of the truth (2 Peter 3:9; 1 Timothy 2:4). That's the whole point of the cross: Jesus died the death we deserved and paid the debt we owed, so we would never have to pay it.

Eternal life starts now

We often think eternal life is only about heaven someday. It includes that—but it begins the moment you believe. In that moment you are forgiven of all your sins, adopted into God's family, and filled with His Spirit to guide and grow you. And when this life ends, death is no longer a dead end but a doorway, where faith becomes sight:

"He will wipe every tear from their eyes. There will be no more death or mourning or crying or pain." (Revelation 21:4)

When Pastor Josh was seven, he decided to run away. He packed a bag, pulled on a big baggy jacket on an 85-degree day, and started down the sidewalk. His dad called his name and asked if he wanted to play one last game of catch on the front lawn before he left. During that game of catch he broke down crying, because in that moment he realized how much his dad loved him. And once he realized it, he didn't want to run away anymore. Many of us keep running from God simply because we don't yet know how good His love is. But "God's kindness is intended to lead you to repentance" (Romans 2:4). His love, not His finger-pointing, is what changes us. And Scripture says His love is better than life itself (Psalm 63:3).

A Takeaway to Sit With

Near the end of the message, our pastor asked a blunt question, and he asked it because he cares: if you died today, what would be your eternal destiny? Have you received God's love by faith in Jesus? That is what determines your destiny. It isn't whether you're a good person or how long you've been going to church; it's what you have done with Jesus. So the question isn't whether you've heard John 3:16 before. It's this: do you know Him? Do you know what it means to be loved by Him, and then to live your life completely in love with Him and for Him?

If God is knocking on the door of your heart, don't turn away. You can pray something as simple as this: "Jesus, thank you that you are real, that you are risen, and that your love is better than life. In this moment, by faith, I open my heart to your love, and for the rest of my life I want to spend it loving you and loving others. In Jesus' name, amen."

If you prayed that for the first time—or recommitted your life to Christ—you've made the best decision of your life. This isn't the finish line of faith; it's the starting line. And you're not meant to walk it alone. If you're anywhere in Livermore or the Tri-Valley, we'd love to help you take your next step and get connected in community. You're welcome here, and we're cheering for you.
