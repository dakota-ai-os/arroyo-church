Pastor Josh Smith opened this message with a challenge: "You were not created to blend in. You were created to stand out. You were not created to follow the crowd. You were created to follow Christ." The problem, he said, is that "there are many chameleon Christians. There are many camouflage Christians." Chameleons "blend into their surroundings but they do not change their surroundings," and the same is true when you wear camo. Many people who call themselves Christians "simply blend in with the world that is around them." There's a lot of good in the world, he said, but also a lot of brokenness and sin, "so if you blend in with the world what that means is you're blending in with sin." By the end of the message he put the question plainly: "Are you standing out in this sinful world? Or are you blending in to this sinful world?"

Continuing our series Grace for You, we worked through Ephesians 5:1-20, and Pastor Josh titled the message "How to Stand Out in a Sinful World." He did not mean standing out in a selfish, look-at-me, social-media way ("look what I've accomplished"), but "in a way that brings glory to God, good to others and even good to yourself." Here are three truths from the passage that help us do exactly that.

1. Let love lead to purity

Paul begins not with a rule but with an identity.

"Follow God's example, therefore, as dearly loved children and walk in the way of love, just as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us as a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God. But among you there must not be even a hint of sexual immorality, or of any kind of impurity, or of greed, because these are improper for God's holy people. Nor should there be obscenity, foolish talk or coarse joking, which are out of place, but rather thanksgiving." (Ephesians 5:1-4)

Notice the order. Before Paul names any sin to avoid, he names who you are: a dearly loved child of God. Who you are is meant to shape how you live. You are not defined by your feelings, your failures, or even your finest moments. You are defined by how your Father sees you in Christ.

From that love, Paul names three sins that, as Pastor Josh put it, "keep us from loving God and loving others":

  • Sexual immorality: at its core, "loving pleasure more than God... loving pleasure more than people." It ends, he said, in "you not loving God and you're not loving the person but you loving yourself."
  • Greed: being possessed by your possessions, believing "enough" will finally satisfy, and neglecting people to chase more.
  • Obscenity and coarse joking: Pastor Josh was clear that Christians should have joy (being a Christian "is the most fun way to live"), but there is a line: "making light of and laughing about things that God doesn't think are funny because it causes damage and destruction to people."

Then comes a word Pastor Josh asked us not to miss: there should "not even be a hint" of these sins among followers of Jesus. Imagine, he said, a delicious cake that someone admits contains "just a hint of deadly poison." You would not keep eating it. Yet with our sin we tend to tolerate a hint: "a little flirt at the office," "a little glance on social media," a hint of pride or selfishness, telling ourselves, "It's just a small sin." Small sins have large consequences. God does not want to be followed partially, or even mostly; He wants your whole heart.

Pastor Josh then read verses 5-6, which he called a warning, and before explaining them he softened them: "This passage isn't talking about believers in Christ that struggle with sin." When we come to Christ, he said, "we're not zapped immediately into perfection. We're on this journey of becoming more like Christ." The warning is about the kind of person who "lives in sin and has no desire to change from it." And even then, "the whole goodness of the gospel is that the doors are open wide and that you can simply walk through by placing your faith in Jesus Christ." So, he said, "Don't reject him, receive him."

How do we actually resist? "The key to living a holy life isn't just you trying really hard to be a better person," Pastor Josh said, "but it's resting in the fact that you are a dearly loved child of God." If you haven't eaten in three days, even a Big Mac looks good. But if you've just had a meal made by a Michelin star chef, you probably won't be drawn to that Big Mac. In the same way, "when you are satisfied in his love and you realize his love is better than life, you won't want to pursue sin because you'll have your savior and you know that having your savior is better than your sin." Love leads to purity.

2. Live in the light

"Therefore do not be partners with them. For you were once darkness, but now you are light in the Lord. Live as children of light... Have nothing to do with the fruitless deeds of darkness, but rather expose them... But everything exposed by the light becomes visible—and everything that is illuminated becomes a light. This is why it is said: 'Wake up, sleeper, rise from the dead, and Christ will shine on you.'" (Ephesians 5:7-14)

Living in the light means two things. First, do not be influenced by those in the dark. This is not a call to abandon relationships with people who do not follow Jesus; we are to be in the world, not of it. It is a call, as Pastor Josh put it, to "not partner with them in their sin." There's a time for boundaries, he said, and even to distance yourself from certain people "so that you can live more for Christ, but in general, we are not to reject people in relationship." Love people; influence them for Christ rather than being pulled into compromise.

Second, let light reveal the truth. Pastor Josh compared sin to the mold that made him sick as a boy, after a leak under the carpet in his family's new house: mold "thrives in the dark," and "your sin thrives when it's hidden." Light exposes it, and light doesn't just reveal; it transforms. That's why, he said, the Bible tells us to confess our sins to one another so we can be healed (James 5:16). And sometimes there's someone you love who needs you to speak the truth in love, "bringing something to the light for them so that they could grow into who God called them to be."

"But if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus, his Son, purifies us from all sin." (1 John 1:7)

"There is no transformation without truth," Pastor Josh said, "and there is no truth without the light shining on the truth." He shared that the moments he has experienced the love of Christ most deeply came when he stepped into the light and his "deepest and darkest sins have been exposed," because "in the midst of my worst sin, God's love was still greater. His grace was still greater."

3. Walk with wisdom

"Be very careful, then, how you live—not as unwise but as wise, making the most of every opportunity, because the days are evil... Do not get drunk on wine, which leads to debauchery. Instead, be filled with the Spirit." (Ephesians 5:15-18)

Knowledge is knowing facts; wisdom is applying them well in real situations. Pastor Josh compared it to soldiers overseas in enemy territory: they can't walk around "like they're in their living room." We live in the middle of a spiritual war, so we cannot stroll through life as if there is no battle.

Paul's warning is specific: "Do not get drunk on wine... Instead, be filled with the Spirit." Pastor Josh was careful here: "being drunk is a sin, but drinking isn't a sin." Jesus' first miracle was turning water into wine at a wedding, "but also we need to be wise about our situation." Then he widened the question: "Everybody is under the influence of someone or something," whether that's alcohol or marijuana, social media and culture, or the wrong people in your life. "We are called not to be under the influence of alcohol, but to be under the influence of the Holy Spirit." So what is influencing you?

The alternative is to be continually filled with the Holy Spirit. You received the Spirit the moment you trusted Christ, but Paul urges an ongoing filling. How? Paul points to worship and gratitude: "always giving thanks to God the Father for everything, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ" (Ephesians 5:20). Pastor Josh compared it to stirring creamer into his wife's coffee. The moment you believe, the creamer is poured in and you are filled, but fixing your heart daily on who Jesus is and what He has done stirs it through until you are truly changed.

A takeaway to carry into your week

If someone investigated your life this week, would there be enough evidence to prove you follow Jesus? Pastor Josh pointed to the moon: it stands out in the night sky, "not with its own light, but through the sun's light going through it." In the same way, we stand out in a dark world not by our own strength but as we are filled with Christ's love and Spirit. For anyone living apart from Christ, his word was hopeful: "Today it's not too late. You can repent, you can give your life to Christ. He can forgive you, he can change you." So ask yourself honestly: What part of my life still needs to come into the light? Whatever it is, bring it to Him today. He sees you with eyes of love.
